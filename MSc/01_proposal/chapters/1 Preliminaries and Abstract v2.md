# ABSTRACT

Smallholder dairy is an important source of regular cash income for rural households in Kenya, and public extension reaches farmers thinly. Digital livestock advisory services such as DigiCow aim to fill part of this gap by giving farmers record-keeping tools, reminders and expert advice on their phones. Evaluations of such services usually report whether yields or income changed, but seldom examine the pathway behind any change or which parts of the service farmers actually use. The independent evaluation of DigiCow under the AgriFin Digital Farmer 2 (ADF2) programme found that DigiCow farmers scored higher on a six-practice cattle health management index than comparison farmers, while differences in milk production and net income from cattle were not statistically significant. This study will use the evaluation's secondary data to (i) estimate the effect of DigiCow participation on milk production per cow, milk sales and annual net income from cattle; (ii) assess the effect of participation on cattle health management practices and examine, through an attenuation decomposition of the practice index and asinh-transformed income, whether any income difference runs through these practices or through other channels; and (iii) examine how the type and intensity of DigiCow information use, and farmer characteristics, are associated with the adoption of recommended practices and reported production change among users. Propensity score matching with a doubly robust check and sensitivity analysis will be used for objective one; attenuation analysis, with causal mediation analysis only where the data and assumptions allow, for objective two; and binary and ordered logit models for objective three, all in R. The pathway results will be reported as suggestive rather than proof of a causal mechanism, because the index measures reported practices rather than clinical animal health. The findings will inform DigiCow and similar providers, county livestock departments and funders designing digital extension for dairy farmers. The expected outputs are an MSc thesis and at least two journal articles.

**Keywords:** digital extension, dairy, cattle health management, attenuation analysis, propensity score matching, Kenya
