**Working title (tentative):** Beyond registration: active use of a mobile dairy advisory service, cattle health management practices and dairy income in Kenya

**Student:** Vicky Jebet (AAE62/49784/2026), MSc Agricultural Information and Communication Management, University of Nairobi. **Supervisor:** Dr Hillary Nyang'anga. November 2026.

## 1. Statement of the problem

Smallholder dairy matters for rural Kenya: the dairy industry contributes about 12% of agricultural gross domestic product and supports an estimated 1.8 million smallholder households (Kenya Dairy Board, 2023). Yet public extension cannot reach most of these farmers in person. The extension officer to farmer ratio was about 1:1,000 in 2019 (Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Irrigation, 2019, p. 140) and had not improved by 2023 (Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development, 2023). Mobile advisory services can fill part of this gap; evidence, mainly from crops, shows average yield gains of about 4% (Fabregas et al., 2019). Their value depends on whether farmers keep receiving, trusting and applying the information, which is an information and communication question.

The independent evaluation of DigiCow, a Kenyan mobile dairy advisory service, had no baseline survey. In it, 74% of registered farmers had accessed the service, but only 33% were active users and 17% reported changing a practice because of it (IDinsight, 2026). DigiCow farmers scored 8.9% higher than comparison farmers on a cattle health management practice index, but their differences in milk production and dairy income were not statistically significant (IDinsight, 2026). The published evaluation compares registered with comparison farmers; it does not report which registered farmers become active users, or how outcomes differ with active use. It is therefore not known which smallholder dairy farmers move from registration to active use of a mobile advisory service, or whether active use, rather than registration, is associated with better cattle health management and higher dairy income.

## 2. Objectives

### 2.1 General objective

To examine the factors associated with active use of DigiCow as a source of dairy information, and the association of active use with cattle health management practices and dairy income, among registered smallholder dairy farmers in Kenya.

### 2.2 Specific objectives

1. To determine the farmer, farm and institutional factors (gender, age or youth status, education, herd size, phone type, cooperative membership and extension contact) associated with active use of DigiCow among registered farmers.
2. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and the adoption of cattle health management practices.
3. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and dairy income.

Active use will follow the dataset's own definition (for example, using DigiCow advice in the past 30 days).

## 3. Hypotheses

1. H~01~: Farmer, farm and institutional factors are not significantly associated with active use of DigiCow.
2. H~02~: Active use of DigiCow is not significantly associated with the adoption of cattle health management practices.
3. H~03~: Active use of DigiCow is not significantly associated with dairy income.

## 4. Data and approach

The study will use farmer-level data from the supervisor's parent project, including the DigiCow evaluation data (502 DigiCow and 775 comparison farmers; IDinsight, 2026). Objective 1 will use a binary logit model of active use. For Objectives 2 and 3, the main comparison is between active and less active registered users, using regression with matching or weighting, with milk yield as a secondary outcome. Because more motivated or better-off farmers may both use the service more and earn more, results will be reported as associations, with sensitivity to unobserved selection. Practices carry costs and lagged returns, so a large short-run income association is not expected. Whether practices are consistent with a pathway from active use to income will be explored as suggestive, not formal mediation. Analysis will be in R.

## 5. Variables to confirm in the dataset

1. How active use is defined and recorded (self-report or platform records), and which content and channel farmers used.
2. Dairy income in actual amounts, milk yield, and the cattle health management practices recorded, separating health practices from reproductive ones such as heat detection.
3. Gender of the decision-maker, age, education, herd size and breed, phone type, cooperative membership, extension contact, feed use and milk market channel.
4. How comparison farmers were selected, survey timing, counties and sample sizes.
