**Working title (tentative):** Beyond registration: active use of a mobile dairy advisory service, cattle health management practices and dairy income in Kenya

**Student:** Vicky Jebet (AAE62/49784/2026), MSc Agricultural Information and Communication Management, University of Nairobi. **Supervisor:** Dr Hillary Nyang'anga.

## 1. Statement of the problem

Smallholder dairy supports an estimated 1.8 million Kenyan households and contributes about 12% of agricultural gross domestic product (Kenya Dairy Board, 2023), yet public extension cannot reach most of these farmers in person: the extension officer to farmer ratio was about 1:1,000 (Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Irrigation, 2019, p. 140). Mobile advisory services can help, with average yield gains of about 4%, mainly in crops (Fabregas et al., 2019), but only if farmers keep using the advice. In the evaluation of DigiCow, a Kenyan mobile dairy advisory service, 74% of registered farmers had accessed the service, but only 33% were active users and 17% had changed a practice because of it. DigiCow farmers scored 8.9% higher on a cattle health management practice index, yet their milk production and dairy income did not differ significantly from comparison farmers (IDinsight, 2026). Because that evaluation compared registered with comparison farmers, it is not known which farmers become active users, or whether active use is associated with better cattle health management and higher dairy income.

## 2. Objectives and hypotheses

**General objective:** To examine the factors associated with active use of DigiCow, and the association of active use with cattle health management practices and dairy income, among registered smallholder dairy farmers in Kenya.

**Specific objectives and working hypotheses**

1. To determine the farmer, farm and institutional factors associated with active use of DigiCow.

   H~01~: Farmer, farm and institutional factors are not significantly associated with active use of DigiCow.
2. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and the adoption of cattle health management practices.

   H~02~: Active use of DigiCow is not significantly associated with the adoption of cattle health management practices.
3. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and dairy income.

   H~03~: Active use of DigiCow is not significantly associated with dairy income.

## 3. Proposed approach

Data are to be obtained from the parent project's farmer-level dataset, subject to a data-use agreement and ethical clearance; the published evaluation surveyed 502 DigiCow and 775 comparison farmers (IDinsight, 2026). Active use (for example, using DigiCow advice in the past 30 days) will be defined from the survey instrument. A logit model will relate active use to gender, age, education, herd size, phone type, cooperative membership and extension contact (Objective 1). Regression will compare active with less active users on practices and dairy income, with milk yield as a secondary outcome (Objectives 2 and 3). Results will be reported as associations, as the data are cross-sectional.
