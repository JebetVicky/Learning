**Working title (tentative):** Beyond registration: active use of a mobile dairy advisory service, cattle health management practices and dairy income in Kenya

**Student:** Vicky Jebet (AAE62/49784/2026), MSc Agricultural Information and Communication Management, University of Nairobi.

## 1. Statement of the problem

Smallholder dairy supports an estimated 1.8 million Kenyan households and contributes about 12% of agricultural gross domestic product (Kenya Dairy Board, 2023). Yet public extension cannot reach most of these farmers in person: the extension officer to farmer ratio was about 1:1,000 (Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Irrigation, 2019, p. 140). Mobile advisory services are promoted to close this gap, and evidence, mainly from crops, shows average yield gains of about 4% (Fabregas et al., 2019). These gains depend on farmers continuing to receive, trust and apply the advice, which makes sustained use an information and communication problem, not only a technical one.

DigiCow, a Kenyan mobile dairy advisory service, shows how large this problem can be. In its independent evaluation, 74% of registered farmers had accessed the service, but only 33% were active users (continuing to engage with the service after registration) and only 17% had changed a practice because of it. DigiCow farmers scored 8.9% higher on a cattle health management practice index, yet their milk production and dairy income did not differ significantly from those of comparison farmers (IDinsight, 2026). Because the evaluation compared registered with comparison farmers, it did not examine which farmers move from registration to active use, or whether those who use the service actively manage cattle health better and earn more from dairy. Without this evidence, service providers and county livestock departments cannot tell whether the absence of a clear income difference reflects low engagement or a weak link between advice, practices and income, and so cannot target support or improve content where it matters.

## 2. Objectives

**General objective:** To examine the factors associated with active use of DigiCow, and the association of active use with cattle health management practices and dairy income, among registered smallholder dairy farmers in Kenya.

**Specific objectives**

1. To determine the farmer, farm and institutional factors associated with active use of DigiCow.
2. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and the adoption of cattle health management practices.
3. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and dairy income.

## 3. Research questions

1. Which farmer, farm and institutional factors are associated with active use of DigiCow?
2. Compared with less active registered users, is active use of DigiCow associated with greater adoption of cattle health management practices?
3. Compared with less active registered users, is active use of DigiCow associated with higher dairy income?
