**Working title (tentative):** Beyond registration: active use of a mobile dairy advisory service, cattle health management practices and dairy income in Kenya

**Student:** Vicky Jebet (AAE62/49784/2026), MSc Agricultural Information and Communication Management, University of Nairobi.

## 1. Statement of the problem

Smallholder dairy supports an estimated 1.8 million Kenyan households and contributes about 12% of agricultural gross domestic product (Kenya Dairy Board, 2023). Keeping cattle healthy and productive depends on timely information on disease prevention and husbandry, yet public extension cannot reach most farmers in person: the extension officer-to-farmer ratio was about 1:1,000 (Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Irrigation, 2019, p. 140).

Mobile advisory services have emerged as one response. Digital agricultural advice has raised yields by about 4% on average, mainly in crops (Fabregas et al., 2019). A farmer may sign up for a service (registration), reach and use it (access), keep engaging with it (active use) and then put its advice into practice (application), and each step can fail. Rigorous evidence that ICT-based extension changes farmers' practices and outcomes remains limited (Aker, 2011). For agricultural information and communication, the unresolved question is therefore not only whether farmers can reach digital advice, but who becomes an active user, and whether active use is associated with applying recommended practices and with higher dairy income.

DigiCow, a Kenyan mobile dairy advisory service, offers a relevant case. Its published evaluation reports that 74% of registered farmers had accessed the service, 33% were active users and 17% reported changing a practice because of it; DigiCow farmers scored 8.9% higher than comparison farmers on a cattle health management practice index, but dairy income did not differ significantly (IDinsight, 2026). Evidence on who becomes an active user, and what active use is associated with, would help providers and county livestock departments target content and support.

## 2. Objectives

**General objective:** To examine the factors associated with active use of DigiCow, and its association with cattle health management practices and dairy income, among registered smallholder dairy farmers in Kenya.

**Specific objectives**

1. To determine the farmer, farm and institutional factors associated with active use of DigiCow.
2. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and the extent of implementing recommended cattle health management practices.
3. To assess the association between active use of DigiCow and dairy income.

## 3. Research questions

1. Which farmer, farm and institutional factors are associated with active use of DigiCow?
2. Among registered farmers, is active use of DigiCow associated with a greater extent of implementing recommended cattle health management practices?
3. Among registered farmers, is active use of DigiCow associated with higher dairy income?
